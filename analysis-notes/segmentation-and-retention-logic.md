# Customer Segmentation & Retention Logic

## Objective
The goal of this analysis is to understand which customers drive profitability, which are at risk of churn, and how retention strategies can be aligned with customer value.

Rather than treating all customers equally, this project focuses on **value-based segmentation** and **behavioral retention patterns**.

---

## 🔹 Why RFM Segmentation Was Used
RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) analysis helps classify customers based on their purchasing behavior.

- **Recency:** How recently a customer made a purchase  
- **Frequency:** How often a customer purchases  
- **Monetary:** How much value the customer contributes  

**Why it matters:**  
RFM allows the business to distinguish:
- High-value loyal customers
- At-risk but previously valuable customers
- Low-engagement, low-value customers

This enables targeted retention strategies instead of generic campaigns.

---

## 🔹 Why Cohort Analysis Was Used
Cohort analysis groups customers based on their first purchase period and tracks their behavior over time.

**Why it matters:**
- Reveals true retention trends
- Separates growth from retention effects
- Helps identify when and why churn occurs

Cohort analysis prevents misleading conclusions that can arise from aggregate metrics alone.

---

## 🔹 Churn Definition Used
A customer is considered churned if they show **no purchase activity beyond the expected repeat window** defined in the business context.

This definition focuses on **behavioral inactivity**, not just account status.

---

## Business Value
Using RFM and cohort analysis together allows decision-makers to:
- Focus retention spend on high-impact customers
- Detect churn risk early
- Improve long-term profitability, not just short-term sales
